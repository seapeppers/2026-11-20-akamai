Managed LLM inference with a reserved GPU pool and serverless burst

Reserve a pool of RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell GPU Linodes sized for your steady traffic. A PackServe-style gateway packs requests onto as few replicas as possible while holding a median per-token latency (TPOT) target. When a spike exceeds the pool, burst-eligible models overflow to Akamai Inference serverless.

How it works

  1. Pack. The gateway predicts each replica's per-token latency and sends the request to the busiest replica that still meets your TPOT target, so the others stay free (and can be released).
  2. Burst. If the pool is full and your model is also in the serverless catalog, the request overflows to Akamai Inference.
  3. Reject. Otherwise you get a fast 429 with Retry-After instead of a long queue.

What we guarantee

  • Routing is tuned for a median TPOT target: 30 ms or 50 ms tiers.
  • Every routing decision is visible in response headers.
  • Burst applies only where you enabled it and only to models that exist in the serverless catalog, so overflow answers come from the same model.
  • Prompts are not stored by telemetry; only aggregate timings are.

What we don't

  • No tail-latency promise. In the research this builds on, P90 TPOT ran well above the median target.
  • Time-to-first-token may rise when packing is aggressive.
  • Pool-only models never burst. The 230B-class model has no serverless equivalent; excess requests are rejected.
  • Serverless capacity, rate limits and pricing are unpublished (beta); burst is best effort.
  • GPU billing is hourly, rounded up; scaling down early saves nothing for the current hour.
  • In this demo, the GPU replicas are SIMULATED.
